### Communion / The Lord's Supper — Study of October 10, 2026

**Original question:** What is the significance of communion?

**Primary passages:** 1 Corinthians 11:23–34; 1 Corinthians 10:16–17; Luke 22:14–20; Matthew 26:26–29.

**Research and discussion notes:**
- **Remembrance:** Jesus instructed His disciples to observe the bread and cup in remembrance of Him (Luke 22:19; 1 Corinthians 11:24–25). Communion recalls His sacrificial death.
- **New Covenant:** The cup is associated with the New Covenant in Jesus' blood (Luke 22:20; 1 Corinthians 11:25), pointing to redemption through Christ.
- **Proclamation and expectation:** Participation proclaims the Lord's death until He comes (1 Corinthians 11:26); communion looks backward to the cross and forward to Christ's return.
- **Fellowship and unity:** The shared bread expresses believers' participation in Christ and unity as one body (1 Corinthians 10:16–17).
- **Self-examination:** Paul calls believers to examine themselves and partake reverently (1 Corinthians 11:27–29). The Corinthian setting also includes divisions and humiliating fellow believers (11:17–22, 33–34); discerning the body should be studied in that context rather than reduced only to treatment of the elements.
- **Passover background:** Jesus instituted the Supper in the context of the Passover meal (Luke 22:14–20), connecting the remembrance of Israel's deliverance with redemption accomplished through Him.
- **Interpretive distinction:** Christian traditions differ on how Christ is present in the bread and cup. The earlier conversation noted these differences without establishing a personal denominational position.
